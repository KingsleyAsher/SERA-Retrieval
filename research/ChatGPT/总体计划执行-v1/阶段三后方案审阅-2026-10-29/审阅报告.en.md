# Review of combined bounds and warm-start candidates

[English](审阅报告.en.md) | [简体中文](审阅报告.md)

This 2026-10-29 review followed stage 3.1 and preceded the next experiment. I reviewed two sources, 30,617 bytes and 523 lines, and recorded 11 constructed mathematical checks. It added no new BGE-M3 retrieval benchmark, certification result or architecture-leadership evidence.

For a covering ball and general center c, `LB_sphere=max(||q−c||−ρ,0)^2`. Orthogonal subspace offset norms in [lo_m,hi_m] yield `LB_stat=Σgap(||(q−c)_m||,[lo_m,hi_m])^2` by reverse triangle inequalities. Their maximum remains a lower bound. I retain both and their separate build/planning/maintenance costs, without adding PQ/OPQ first.

Unit members do not imply a unit centroid. For B={(1,0),(−1,0)}, c=0 and q=(0,1), the true center squared distance is 1, not 2. With only q unit, use `1+||c||²−2q·c`. Renormalizing the center requires rebuilding its radius/statistics. The subspace proof uses an inequality between squared norm differences and vector differences, not equality: orthogonal unit vectors have norm difference zero but squared vector distance 2.

Neither bound universally wins. For B={(±7/25,0,24/25),(0,±7/25,24/25)} and q=(2/3,2/3,1/3), all points are unit, sphere LB≈.726036, statistic LB≈.691733 and true minimum≈.986667. For B={(0,±1)}, q=(1,0), sphere LB=0 while statistic LB=true minimum=2. These are constructed counterexamples, not the distribution of BGE vectors.

Valid overlapping intervals mean unresolved order, not failed refinement. True equality implies overlapping enclosing closed intervals, but overlap does not imply equality. Continue refinement or exact dyadic comparison. A supported online Kth distance .8 may safely prune less than the offline true Kth distance .2; inequality with the oracle is not itself an error. Require K distinct legal supports and conservative directions. Cosine-to-L2 conversion needs exact unit assumptions; do not take cos of an already cosine score. Define slack and all category denominators consistently. Sampling zero violations does not prove every floating input safe.

DeepSeek's S1 can score legal seeds, change search order and skip scored vectors, but stopping must cover every unvisited region. If the queued subset has upper .7, current threshold .8, and another region has upper .99 with a real .95 item, considering only the first queue stops incorrectly. Order keys may be heuristics; they are not valid whole-region upper bounds. Compare order and stopping separately.

S2 can migrate bounds with βΔ and K supported old scores; it does not require old global exact top-K. A fixed similarity window is unjustified. With q0=(1,0), q=(.88,sqrt(1−.88²)), support x=(.5,sqrt(.75)) and tail y=(−1,0), Δ≈.489898 exceeds .45, yet `−1+Δ<.5−Δ` still excludes the tail. Margins, supports and blocks matter, not Δ alone.

Equal-list-size/nlist≈sqrt(N) tables for 1,213/10,000/50,000 are algebraic estimates, not measured scale tiers or grounds to reject small libraries. Zero new corpus-vector visits still leave old-query, metadata, matching, support and later scan costs. Unproved float bounds may support labeled diagnostics, not lossless claims.

The stage-3.1 evidence remains ANN50≈4.179 ms/100% overlap; seed C200≈8.043 ms/96.25% mean/70% minimum; merged C200≈8.361 ms/100%, with average native distance work still 606.625. I retained the corrected ideas for a finite compact-layout/IVF experiment and kept strict certification separate. No next part was opened by this review alone.
