# Writing and maintaining the two editions

[English](LANGUAGES.md) | [简体中文](LANGUAGES.zh-CN.md)

I write the Chinese and English editions as original project documentation. Both start from the same research facts and intended claims; neither is a translation pipeline output or a sentence-by-sentence template of the other. Structure and phrasing may differ, but prerequisites, formulas, numbers, negative results and current status must agree.

The archive preserves the dated Chinese research records and adds English accounts written for an English-reading audience. Public editorial changes remove assistant-session framing and use my perspective as project owner. Proposals from DeepSeek and WorkBuddy retain attribution; their own claims do not become verified project results. Older stopping points stay explicitly historical.

Each prose document has a language counterpart and navigation. Canonical code, schemas, identifiers, hashes, measurements and citation metadata stay shared, with complete bilingual artifact inventories. This avoids creating divergent executable or numeric versions. Apache's official license is preserved rather than rewritten as an original legal instrument; a Chinese project guide explains my licensing decision.

When I change a claim, I update both editions and the relevant shared evidence. Coverage checking verifies paired files and navigation, while targeted factual checks cover the research status and headline comparisons. File coverage alone does not prove every passage semantically equivalent or establish a new experiment.
